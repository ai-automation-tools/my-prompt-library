# Meeting Audit and Decline Scripts

## Purpose
Audit a recurring meeting load against what each meeting produces, and write the scripts that make declining or reshaping one survivable.

## Instructions
Act as an operations coach. Audit the calendar below and produce the conversations needed to change it.

Inputs:
- **Recurring meetings:** [name, frequency, duration, attendee count, your role in each]
- **Ad hoc meeting load:** [hours per week]
- **Your outputs:** [what you are actually accountable for producing]
- **Organizational context:** [seniority, culture around meetings, who convenes what]

For each meeting classify: decision, information, coordination, creation, or relationship. Then assess:
- **The output test.** What would be worse next week if it did not happen? An answer of "we would be less aligned" is not an output.
- **Your marginal contribution.** A meeting can be valuable and still not need you. These are different findings with different remedies.
- **Format fit.** Information meetings are usually documents. Decision meetings need a decider present and a pre-read. Coordination meetings need the smallest attendee set that covers the dependencies.
- **Cost.** Attendee count times duration times frequency, annualized in hours. The number is usually the argument.

Then write the scripts, because the audit is the easy part:
- Declining an invitation while offering the information the convener actually needed
- Proposing asynchronous replacement without implying the meeting was worthless
- Leaving a standing meeting you have attended for years
- Asking a more senior convener for an agenda or a pre-read
- Shortening a meeting rather than cancelling it, as the fallback when cancelling is not available

Keep each script short and free of justification stacking — a decline with four reasons reads as negotiable.

## Output Format
- Meeting audit table with classification, output test, and annualized cost
- Recommendations: keep, shorten, asynchronous, delegate, decline
- Scripts per scenario
- The two changes to make first
