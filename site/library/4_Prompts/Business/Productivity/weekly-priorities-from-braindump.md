# Weekly Priorities from a Brain Dump

## Purpose
Turn an unsorted list of everything on your mind into a week that fits, with the cuts made explicitly rather than discovered on Friday.

## Instructions
Act as a planning partner. Work through the dump below.

Inputs:
- **Brain dump:** [paste everything, unsorted, including the vague items]
- **Fixed commitments this week:** [meetings, travel, obligations, with hours]
- **Real available hours:** [after the fixed commitments]
- **Deadlines:** [with which are external and which are self-imposed]
- **Last week's carryover:** [what did not get done, and how long it has been carrying]

Process:
1. **Separate tasks from projects.** Anything needing more than one action is a project and belongs on the list only as its next action. This is where most weekly plans quietly fail — a project on a task list never gets started.
2. **Name the vague ones.** An item like "the budget thing" costs energy every time it is read and cannot be started. Convert each into a concrete next action or delete it.
3. **Estimate each task**, then apply a multiplier to the total based on how last week's estimates actually landed. Use the observed ratio, not optimism.
4. **Compare the estimated total to available hours.** If it exceeds them — it will — cut until it fits. Cutting is the deliverable here, not a failure of the process.
5. **Choose three outcomes** for the week that would make it a good week even if nothing else happened.
6. **Handle the carryover** explicitly: an item that has carried three weeks is not a scheduling problem. Do it, delegate it, schedule it with a date, or delete it.

## Output Format
- Three outcomes for the week
- Scheduled tasks fitted to real hours, with estimates
- The cut list, stated as a decision with its consequence
- Carryover verdicts
- Projects with their next actions extracted
