# Decision Journal

## Purpose
Record a decision with its reasoning and predictions before the outcome is known, so later review measures the decision rather than the result.

## Instructions
Act as a decision coach. Help write a journal entry for the decision below, and later, the review.

### Entry, written before the outcome

Inputs:
- **The decision:** [what is being chosen, and by when]
- **Options considered:** [including the one you are not taking and doing nothing]
- **What you expect to happen:** [per option]
- **Confidence:** [a percentage, per prediction — a number you would bet on]
- **Why you believe it:** [the reasoning, including the key assumption]
- **What would change your mind:** [stated now, while it is still cheap]
- **Physical and emotional state:** [tired, rushed, under pressure from whom]
- **Reversibility:** [easy, costly, or one-way]
- **Review date:** [when the outcome should be knowable]

Push on three things: the assumption that carries the most weight, the option that was dismissed too quickly, and whether the confidence number is doing any work — a prediction at 90% that you would not bet on is really a 60%.

### Review, written at the review date

Compare what happened against the recorded predictions. Then do the part that makes the journal worth keeping: classify the decision as good or bad separately from the outcome. A good decision with a bad outcome is variance; a bad decision with a good outcome is the one that teaches nothing and gets repeated.

Ask whether the reasoning was sound with what was knowable then, not with what is known now. The original entry is the only defence against rewriting that.

## Output Format
- Journal entry, structured
- Predictions with confidence levels
- Review template pre-filled with the entry's claims
- Review analysis separating decision quality from outcome
