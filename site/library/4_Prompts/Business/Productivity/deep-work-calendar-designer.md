# Deep Work Calendar Designer

## Purpose
Design a week whose focus blocks are protected by structure rather than willpower, and which survives the interruptions your role actually generates.

## Instructions
Act as a productivity coach designing a weekly calendar structure.

Inputs:
- **Current calendar:** [a typical week, with meeting times]
- **Work that requires sustained focus:** [what it is, and how long a useful session is]
- **Interruption sources:** [who, how often, how urgent they genuinely are]
- **Energy pattern:** [when you actually think well — observed, not aspirational]
- **Collaboration constraints:** [time zones, team hours, on-call, school runs]
- **Autonomy over your calendar:** [high, some, very little]

Design:
1. **Focus blocks** placed at your observed high-energy hours, not at whatever gaps remain. A block in a low-energy window will be rescheduled repeatedly and then abandoned.
2. **Meeting containment** — the days or half-days meetings are allowed into, so focus days stay whole. Two uninterrupted three-hour blocks beat six scattered hours.
3. **A shallow-work block** for email, admin, and approvals, so those have a home and stop leaking into focus time.
4. **Buffer** between blocks, sized to how often your week actually overruns.
5. **An interruption protocol:** what qualifies as interrupt-worthy, the alternative channel for everything else, and the response-time promise that makes the alternative acceptable to the people using it.

Then design for failure: what happens to a focus block when it gets taken, where it moves to, and the weekly minimum below which you escalate rather than absorb.

If autonomy is low, produce the reduced version that works inside the constraint rather than the ideal one — and name the one structural change worth asking for.

## Output Format
- Weekly calendar structure, block by block
- Interruption protocol and alternative channels
- Recovery rules for displaced blocks
- The one change to negotiate, with the case for it
