# Inbox Triage System

## Purpose
Build a triage system that separates deciding from doing, since the cost of email is mostly the repeated re-reading of things already read.

## Instructions
Act as a productivity coach. Design an email triage system for the situation below.

Inputs:
- **Daily volume:** [messages received, and roughly how many need action]
- **Role:** [what people actually email you about]
- **Client:** [Gmail, Outlook, other — the automation available differs]
- **Current state:** [inbox count, how often you check, what goes wrong]
- **Response time expectations:** [real ones, from your role and not your anxiety]

Design:
1. **Filters that run before you see anything.** Automated notifications, newsletters, and CC-only threads should never enter the triage queue. Most volume problems are a filtering problem wearing an attention problem's clothes.
2. **A triage pass** that only decides — delete, delegate, do-now if under two minutes, defer with a date, or reference — and never drafts a considered reply. Mixing the two is what makes a triage pass take an hour.
3. **A separate processing block** where the deferred items get done, scheduled, not squeezed.
4. **Checking schedule:** how many times a day, at what times, with the notification settings that make that schedule survive contact with reality.
5. **The exception channel** for genuinely urgent things, agreed with the people who need it, so the inbox does not have to be monitored as if everything might be.

Include the defaults that reduce inbound: when to reply-all, when to use a channel other than email, and the three recurring threads that should become a document or a recurring meeting agenda item instead.

## Output Format
- Filter rules, client-specific
- Triage decision tree
- Daily schedule with block lengths
- Inbound-reduction changes, ranked by volume removed
