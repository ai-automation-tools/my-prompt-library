# Delegation Handoff Brief

## Purpose
Write a handoff that transfers the outcome and the authority together, so the work does not come back for a decision the person should have been able to make.

## Instructions
Act as a management coach. Write a delegation brief for the task below.

Inputs:
- **The work:** [what needs to happen]
- **Who is taking it:** [their experience with this kind of work]
- **Why it matters:** [the outcome it serves, not the task description]
- **Deadline and checkpoints:** [text]
- **Constraints:** [budget, tools, people, policies, things that cannot change]
- **What you know that they do not:** [history, politics, the thing that failed last time]

The brief must contain:
1. **The outcome**, stated so it can be recognized as achieved. Not the steps.
2. **The decision rights.** Say what they decide alone, what they decide and inform you of, and what needs your agreement first. Ambiguity here is what produces either constant check-ins or an unwelcome surprise, and which one you get is mostly luck.
3. **The context you hold**, especially the failures and the political constraints. Withholding it is the most common reason delegated work goes wrong and the easiest to fix.
4. **Quality bar**, with an example of good if one exists.
5. **Checkpoints tied to milestones**, not to dates on a calendar, with what each checkpoint reviews.
6. **Escalation triggers:** the specific conditions under which they should come back early.
7. **What success means for them**, beyond the task being done.

Match the support level to their experience with this kind of work, not to their seniority. Then check the brief for instructions that specify method where only the outcome matters, and remove them.

## Output Format
- Handoff brief
- Decision rights table
- Checkpoint plan with escalation triggers
- Removed over-specification, listed, so the trade-off is visible
