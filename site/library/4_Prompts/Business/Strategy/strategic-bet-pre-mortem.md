# Strategic Bet Pre-Mortem

## Purpose
Stress-test a strategic decision before committing to it by assuming it has already failed, then working backwards to the causes, early warnings, and cheap mitigations.

## Instructions
Act as a skeptical advisor who has seen similar bets fail. Assume it is [time horizon, e.g. 18 months] from now and the bet below has clearly failed. Don't ask whether it could fail; explain how it did. Be specific to this situation. A generic risk like "poor execution" is not an answer; name who didn't do what, and why.

Inputs:
- **The bet:** [what you are about to commit to, in two or three sentences]
- **What success looks like:** [a measurable outcome and date]
- **Resources committed:** [money, people, time, and what else they would otherwise do]
- **Key assumptions:** [what must be true for this to work]
- **Who decides and who executes:** [roles]

Steps:
1. **Write the failure story** — three distinct, plausible ways it failed, each as a short narrative with a cause, not a list of risks. Make them different in kind (e.g. market, execution, and an external shock), not three versions of one.
2. **Trace each to an assumption** — name the assumption from the inputs that broke, or add one the inputs left unstated.
3. **Find the earliest signal** — for each failure, the first observable sign, how soon it would show, and who would see it. A signal nobody is watching is not a signal.
4. **Rate each** — likelihood and impact on a 1–5 scale, with one sentence of reasoning, and flag any failure that would be irreversible or only visible after the money is spent.
5. **Propose the cheapest mitigation** — a test, a staged commitment, a kill criterion, or a change in scope, preferring steps that cost days rather than months.
6. **Set kill and pivot criteria** — the specific metric and threshold, and the date, at which you stop or change course, written now while nobody is attached to the outcome.

## Output Format
- Three failure stories, each with the broken assumption
- A table: failure, earliest signal, owner of the signal, likelihood, impact, mitigation
- Kill and pivot criteria with thresholds and review dates
- A verdict: proceed, proceed with changes, or don't, with the single biggest reason
