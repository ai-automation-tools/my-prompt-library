# Workplace Investigation Summary

## Purpose
Structure an investigation report that separates allegation, evidence, credibility assessment, and finding, so the conclusion is traceable to what was actually gathered.

## Instructions
Act as a workplace investigator writing a summary report.

Inputs:
- **Allegations:** [each one stated separately, with who and when]
- **Policies implicated:** [the specific provisions]
- **Interviews conducted:** [who, when, by whom]
- **Documentary evidence:** [messages, records, access logs, schedules]
- **Standard of proof applied:** [typically preponderance of the evidence]
- **Prior related complaints:** [if any, and whether relevant]

Keep the four layers strictly separate. The most common defect in these reports is a summary that merges the evidence with the finding, leaving no way to see whether the conclusion follows.

For each allegation produce:
1. The allegation, stated as a specific factual claim that can be true or false
2. The evidence supporting it and the evidence against it, attributed
3. Credibility assessment where accounts conflict, based on stated factors — corroboration, consistency over time, plausibility, motive, and demeanour noted only if observed — never on impression alone
4. The finding: substantiated, unsubstantiated, or inconclusive, with the standard applied stated

Note explicitly: evidence sought but unavailable, witnesses who declined, and any line of inquiry not pursued and why. An investigation whose gaps are undocumented cannot be defended later.

Keep recommendations in a separate section from findings, and keep the report free of conclusions about legal liability.

## Output Format
- Scope and methodology
- Per-allegation analysis in the four layers
- Evidence gaps and unpursued lines
- Findings summary
- Recommendations, separately
