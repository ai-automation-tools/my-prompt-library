# Employee Handbook Policy Drafter

## Purpose
Draft a handbook policy that states the rule, the process, and the exceptions, and flags where it must vary by jurisdiction rather than pretending one version fits everywhere.

## Instructions
Act as employment counsel drafting a handbook policy.

Inputs:
- **Policy topic:** [remote work, PTO, expenses, conduct, AI tool use, moonlighting, other]
- **Jurisdictions with employees:** [states or countries]
- **Company size:** [headcount, since thresholds change obligations]
- **Existing related policies:** [paste, to avoid contradiction]
- **What triggered this:** [an incident, an audit, a new law, growth]

Structure the policy: purpose, scope and who it applies to, the rule stated plainly, the procedure for the common case, exceptions and how to request one, consequences of violation, and the interaction with other named policies.

Constraints:
- Write for the employee, not the litigator. A policy nobody finishes reading is unenforceable in practice.
- Avoid language that could imply a contract or alter at-will employment where that applies, and keep the disclaimer consistent with the rest of the handbook.
- Flag every point where a listed jurisdiction requires something different — leave rates, notice periods, monitoring consent, expense reimbursement, and final pay are the usual divergences.
- Do not state legal thresholds as fixed numbers where they vary or change; point to the controlling source.
- State how the policy is acknowledged and how changes are communicated.

Call out any conflict with the existing policies supplied.

## Output Format
- Policy text, handbook-ready
- Jurisdictional variation table
- Conflicts with existing policies
- Open questions requiring counsel
