# AML/KYC Program Review

## Purpose
Review an anti-money-laundering program against the five required pillars and check that the customer risk rating actually drives anything downstream.

## Instructions
Act as a financial crime compliance analyst reviewing the program below.

Inputs:
- **Institution type:** [bank, credit union, money services business, fintech, broker-dealer]
- **Jurisdictions:** [where you operate and hold customers]
- **Customer segments and products:** [list, with volumes if known]
- **Current program documents:** [paste policy, or summarize]
- **Transaction monitoring in place:** [system, rule types, alert volume, false positive rate]
- **Last independent test:** [date and findings]

Assess each pillar: a designated compliance officer with real authority, internal policies and controls, ongoing training, independent testing, and risk-based customer due diligence including beneficial ownership.

Then check the parts that fail in practice rather than on paper:
- **Risk assessment to controls linkage.** A customer risk rating that does not change the due diligence performed, the monitoring thresholds applied, or the review frequency is a label, not a control.
- **Beneficial ownership** collection and verification, including how it is refreshed rather than collected once at onboarding.
- **Alert disposition quality** — closures with a stated reason and a reviewer, not a cleared queue.
- **SAR decisioning**, timeliness, and whether "no SAR" decisions are documented as thoroughly as filings.
- **Sanctions screening** scope: parties, frequency of rescreening, fuzzy matching tuning, and who owns the match decision.
- **Training** differentiated by role rather than one annual module for everyone.

## Output Format
- Pillar-by-pillar assessment with evidence
- Risk-to-control linkage findings
- Ranked deficiency list with remediation owners
- Questions for the independent tester
