# Incident and Near-Miss Report

## Purpose
Write up a safety incident or near miss so the report leads to a changed condition rather than to a retraining record.

## Instructions
Act as an EHS professional. Write the report from the facts below. Keep it blameless: the person involved is a source of information about how the work is actually done, not a cause. Use only supplied facts; mark anything else `[to be confirmed]`.

Inputs:
- **What happened:** [the event, in the reporter's words]
- **Classification:** [near miss, first aid, medical treatment, lost time, property damage]
- **When and where:** [date, time, shift, area, equipment]
- **People involved:** [roles, experience, and time into the shift — not names in the shared report]
- **Conditions:** [task being performed, PPE in use, equipment state, staffing, lighting, time pressure]
- **Immediate actions taken:** [what was done in the first hour]

Produce:
1. **Summary** — three sentences, factual, no interpretation
2. **Sequence** — what happened step by step, with the last point at which the outcome was still avoidable identified explicitly
3. **Conditions present** — equipment, procedure, training, staffing, environment, time pressure, and any recent change to any of them
4. **Contributing factors** — grouped as equipment, procedure, training, supervision, and organizational. Every incident has factors in more than one group; a report naming only one is incomplete
5. **The gap between procedure and practice** — what the procedure says, what the work actually requires, and why the difference exists. This is usually where the real finding is
6. **Potential severity** — what the worst realistic outcome of this same event would have been, which is how a near miss gets the attention it deserves
7. **Corrective actions** — ordered by the hierarchy of controls: elimination, substitution, engineering, administrative, PPE. An action at the administrative or PPE level must state why a higher control was not feasible
8. **Read-across** — other areas, shifts and equipment with the same exposure

No action may be "retrain the operator" as the sole corrective action, and no finding may be "operator error".

## Output Format
- Incident report in the sections above
- Contributing factors table by category
- Action table: action, control level, owner, due date, verification
- A read-across list with owners
