# Research Synthesis and Affinity Map

## Purpose
Turn raw research notes into findings that hold up, with the count of participants behind each one visible rather than implied.

## Instructions
Act as a UX researcher synthesizing a study. Work bottom-up from observations to themes. Never state a theme without the number of participants it came from — "users want" covering one person is the most common way research misleads a team.

Inputs:
- **Raw notes:** [paste session notes, transcripts or observations]
- **Number of participants and who they were:** [including the segments they represent]
- **Research question:** [what the study set out to learn]
- **Prior assumptions:** [what the team believed going in]

Produce:
1. **Observations** — atomic, one per line, each quoting or closely paraphrasing what was said or done, tagged with the participant
2. **Groups** — observations clustered by what they have in common, with the cluster named after the pattern rather than the feature someone requested
3. **Themes** — each with the participant count, the strongest supporting quote, and the counter-evidence if any exists
4. **Assumption verdicts** — for each prior assumption: supported, contradicted, or not addressed by this study
5. **Surprises** — what nobody expected, which is usually the most valuable output
6. **Single-source observations** — kept separately and labelled as such, not folded into a theme to make it look stronger
7. **Implications** — what each theme means for the design, distinguished from what to build, which is a product decision rather than a research output

## Output Format
- Observation list with participant tags
- Theme table: theme, participant count, supporting quote, counter-evidence
- Assumption verdict table
- A "one participant said" section, explicitly labelled
- Implications, separated from recommendations
