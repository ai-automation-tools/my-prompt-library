# Jobs-to-be-Done Interview Analysis

## Purpose
Reconstruct why someone switched, in the sequence it actually happened, so the design responds to the forces that moved them rather than to a stated preference.

## Instructions
Act as a product researcher applying a jobs-to-be-done lens. Analyse the switch interview below. Build the timeline first and interpret second — the value is in the sequence, and interpretation applied too early flattens it.

Inputs:
- **Interview transcript or notes:** [paste]
- **Product they switched to:** [what they now use]
- **Product or method they left:** [what they used before]

Reconstruct:
1. **First thought** — the moment the old way first became noticeably inadequate, and what was happening around it
2. **Passive looking** — what they noticed but did not act on, and for how long
3. **The triggering event** — what turned looking into acting, stated as a specific event on a specific occasion
4. **Active looking** — what they compared, where they looked, who they asked
5. **The deciding moment** — what tipped it, and what almost stopped them
6. **First use** — what they expected, what actually happened, and whether the expectation held
7. **The four forces** — push from the old situation, pull of the new, anxiety about the new, habit holding them to the old; each with the quote that evidences it
8. **The job statement** — written as a situation, motivation and desired outcome, in the participant's language, not in feature terms

Mark everything not evidenced in the transcript as an inference. A job statement built from what the researcher expected is the standard failure mode here.

## Output Format
- Timeline with a quote at each stage
- Forces table: force, evidence, strength
- One job statement in situation-motivation-outcome form
- Design implications, each traced to a force
