# User Interview Guide

## Purpose
Write an interview guide that gets at what people actually did, rather than what they predict they would do.

## Instructions
Act as a UX researcher. Write an interview guide for the study below. Every question must be about a specific past event. Reject any question of the form "would you use" or "how much would you pay" — those answers are unreliable and will be treated as data anyway.

Inputs:
- **Research question:** [what decision this informs]
- **Participants:** [who they are, how they were recruited, how many]
- **Session length:** [minutes]
- **What is already believed:** [the assumptions this study should be able to disprove]

Produce:
1. **Opening** — consent, recording, the framing that there are no wrong answers, and an explicit statement that you did not build the thing being discussed
2. **Warm-up** — their role and context, two or three questions
3. **The last time** — walk through the most recent occurrence of the behaviour in question, in order, with the detail of what they had open, who else was involved, and what went wrong
4. **Workarounds** — what they have rigged up, which is where unmet need shows most clearly
5. **Probes** — the follow-ups that turn a general statement into a specific one: "tell me about the last time", "what happened next", "what did you do instead"
6. **Closing** — anything they expected to be asked, referrals

For each section, state the assumption it is testing. Mark the two or three questions that would most change the team's mind if answered unexpectedly.

## Output Format
- Guide with timings per section
- Assumption-to-question map
- A list of leading questions deliberately avoided, with the neutral version alongside
